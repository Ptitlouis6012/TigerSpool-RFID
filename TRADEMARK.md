# Trademark policy

**Short version: build them, sell them, no fee, no registration. Run the official
firmware and you may call it a TigerSpool. Change the firmware and call it
something else.**

## What this covers

The **code** in this repository is MIT-licensed ([LICENSE](LICENSE)) — free to
use, modify, distribute and sell, with no conditions beyond keeping the notice.

The **names** are different. **TigerTag** (the RFID protocol and the cloud
service) and **TigerSpool** (this product) are trademarks of the TigerTag
Project. This page is the whole policy for using them.

## Make and sell

**Anyone may manufacture and sell TigerSpool hardware.** No license fee, no
registration, no permission needed.

1. Build to [hardware/BOM.md](hardware/BOM.md) and [docs/WIRING.md](docs/WIRING.md).
2. Flash the official firmware, unmodified.
3. Ship it.

The one condition for calling your product a **TigerSpool** is that it runs the
**official firmware, unmodified**. That is not about control — it is what lets a
buyer assume that any TigerSpool works the same way, gets the same updates, and
talks to the rest of the TigerTag ecosystem. A device that says TigerSpool and
behaves differently makes the name worth less to everyone, including you.

## Forks

**Forks are welcome. Use a different name.**

Modify anything you like — the MIT license grants that. Just do not ship it as a
"TigerSpool", and do not name it in a way that implies it is one or is endorsed
by the project.

Fine: *"SpoolBridge, based on TigerSpool"* · *"compatible with TigerTag"*

Not fine: *"TigerSpool Pro"* · *"TigerSpool by <company>"* · anything using the
TigerTag logo as your product's mark

## Using the names in writing

You may always use the names to state a fact accurately: "compatible with
TigerTag", "based on TigerSpool", "reads TigerTag chips". That is nominative use
and it needs no permission.

You may not use them as, or as part of, your own product name, company name or
domain, or in a way that suggests the project produced or endorsed something it
did not.

## Logos

TigerTag logos and brand assets are **not** covered by the MIT license. Use them
to refer to the project; do not use them as your own product's identity, and do
not modify them.
